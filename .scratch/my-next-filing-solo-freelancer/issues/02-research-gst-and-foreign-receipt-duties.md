# Research GST and foreign-receipt duties for solo freelancers

Type: research
Status: resolved
Blocked by: none
Research branch: `research/solo-freelancer-gst-exports`

## Question

Using current official Indian sources, which GST and adjacent statutory duties can safely enter a filing workspace for resident-individual solo digital freelancers with domestic or Foreign professional receipts? Establish registration thresholds and aggregate-turnover treatment, export-of-service conditions, place of supply, receipt-currency conditions, LUT or bond behavior, zero-rating, invoice and return obligations, GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B or QRMP calendars, platform-mediated work, mixed domestic and foreign receipts, and any material RBI, FEMA, SOFTEX, or realization duty. Separate supported dated Obligations from guidance and explicit exclusions. Write the findings to `../research/gst-and-foreign-receipt-duties.md` with primary-source citations.

## Answer

[GST and foreign-receipt duties for solo freelancers](../research/gst-and-foreign-receipt-duties.md) establishes a narrow safe boundary: one resident individual supplies their own remote digital service from one Indian state, has no other business, uses no more than one active normal-taxpayer GSTIN, can identify the overseas contractual recipient, satisfies every export-of-service condition, and has no unclear agency, special-place-of-supply, platform-fee reverse-charge, or receipt-route fact.

Qualifying exports count in PAN-wide GST aggregate turnover. The service threshold remains ₹20 lakh, or ₹10 lakh in Manipur, Mizoram, Nagaland, and Tripura. Below-threshold inter-State service and qualifying e-commerce service suppliers retain notified registration exemptions. A registered normal taxpayer can have monthly GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B Obligations, or a complete QRMP calendar with conditional PMT-06 reviews; IFF is optional and 2026-27 GSTR-9 needs later period-specific review.

Foreign-service duties change on 1 October 2026. The earlier RBI regime uses a nine-month realization period and SOFTEX for qualifying software exports. FEMA 23(R)/2026-RB then introduces general service EDF declarations and 15-month or 18-month realization periods. Annual totals can support income and aggregate-turnover declarations, but they cannot produce invoice, LUT-first-export, EDF/SOFTEX, realization, or Rule 96A dates. Those items need narrower records or must remain guidance. Every GST extension and the uncommenced intermediary amendment require a release-time source review.
