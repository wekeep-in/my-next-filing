# Research income-tax treatment for solo digital freelancers

Type: research
Status: resolved
Blocked by: none
Research branch: `research/solo-freelancer-income-tax`

## Question

Using current official Indian sources for Tax Year 2026-27, which income-tax calculation paths can safely support resident-individual solo digital freelancers such as developers, designers, writers, marketers, and consultants who serve domestic or foreign clients directly or through freelance platforms? Establish profession and business classifications, presumptive-method eligibility and limits, gross-receipt treatment, platform fees, foreign-currency conversion, timing, cash tests, tax withholding, advance tax, annual return obligations, and the exact facts that must stop calculation. Treat foreign-tax credit and unrelated income as excluded. Write the findings to `../research/income-tax-treatment.md` with primary-source citations.

## Answer

[Income-tax treatment for solo digital freelancers](../research/income-tax-treatment.md) finds that a foreign client does not itself disqualify a resident individual from section 58 presumptive taxation. The safe existing path remains a user-confirmed section 62 specified profession at 50% of gross receipts. Broader designers, writers, marketers, and generic consultants cannot be assigned to that path by job title; supporting them may require the distinct 6%/8% eligible-business path, its receipt-mode split, and its five-year exclusion history.

Use gross receipts before platform fees and Indian withholding. Accept only a user-confirmed annual integer-rupee total already prepared from tax records under the user's regular accounting method. Net platform payouts, unresolved currency conversion, open receivables, exchange adjustments, and foreign withholding must stop the proposed expansion. Foreign clients alone, platform use alone, and actual Indian TDS/TCS alone are not stop facts.

Advance tax remains due in full by 15 March 2027 for either supported presumptive path when the liability threshold is met. Return filing has independent triggers beyond income, and section 58 eligibility does not establish ITR-4 eligibility. Foreign assets, accounts, signing authority, source-of-income classification, and foreign-tax relief can require different return guidance. The rule and source set must remain expiry-bound and be rechecked before release.
