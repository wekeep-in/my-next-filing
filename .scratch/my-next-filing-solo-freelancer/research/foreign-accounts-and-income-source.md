# Foreign payment accounts and income-source classification

Reviewed on 2 September 2026 for Tax Year 2026-27 (1 April 2026 to
31 March 2027). This note uses current official Indian primary sources. It is
product-boundary research, not tax, FEMA, or legal advice.

## Answer

A foreign client, foreign currency, or foreign-branded payment provider does
not by itself establish either a foreign asset or income from a source outside
India. The legal arrangement does.

The first safe foreign-client path is narrow:

- the user is resident and ordinarily resident in India for income-tax
  purposes;
- the freelance profession is set up and managed in India, and all work that
  earns the receipts is performed while the user is physically in India;
- the client or platform pays through an authorised cross-border payment route
  into the user's own account with a bank in India;
- the user has no account, local receiving account, wallet, or provider balance
  outside India, and no authority to operate somebody else's account outside
  India; and
- no foreign tax was withheld or paid and no foreign-tax relief is claimed.

On those facts, the Application may include the confirmed gross rupee receipts
in the supported professional-income calculation. It should not call them
foreign-source income merely because the customer is abroad. This is a
conservative inference from the place and setup of the income-producing
professional operations, not an official ruling on every remote-services
contract.

A foreign bank account in the user's name, or authority to operate any account
outside India, is a clear stop for simplified return guidance. A balance held
under a foreign wallet, freelance platform, or virtual receiving arrangement
is less categorical: it may be a foreign depository account, another foreign
account, or an asset represented by a claim against a foreign entity. The
reviewed authorities do not map product brands or marketing terms to those tax
categories. When the user cannot establish the legal facts from their account
documents, the Application must preserve any otherwise valid tax estimate but
stop the return-form and foreign-disclosure conclusion.

## Why the return form changes

Rule 164 makes SUGAM (ITR-4) available for a resident other than not ordinarily
resident who uses a section 58 presumptive method, subject to exclusions. A
person is not eligible for ITR-4 if they have an asset, including a financial
interest in an entity, located outside India; signing authority in an account
outside India; income from a source outside India; or a foreign-tax relief or
deduction claim. An individual with business or professional income who falls
outside ITR-4 uses ITR-3. [Income-tax Rules, 2026, rule 164, notified 20 March
2026 and effective 1 April
2026](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/documents/d/guest/notification-22-2026-1)

Section 263 separately requires a resident other than not ordinarily resident
to furnish a return, regardless of income or loss, if at any time during the
tax year the person:

- holds, beneficially or otherwise, an asset or financial interest located
  outside India;
- has signing authority in an account located outside India; or
- is a beneficiary of a foreign asset, subject to the stated exception where
  its income is included in the beneficial owner's income.

There is no value threshold in this filing trigger. Section 263 defines a
beneficial owner as an individual who directly or indirectly provided
consideration for an asset held for that individual's or another person's
benefit, and a beneficiary as an individual who derives benefit during the tax
year where someone else provided the consideration. [Income-tax Act, 2025 as
amended by Finance Act, 2026, sections 263(1)(a)(ix), 263(1)(b), and
263(9), consolidation issued 30 March
2026](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/documents/d/guest/income_tax_act_2025_as_amended_by_fa_act_2026-pdf)

The latest complete ITR-3 schedule available for a finished period is useful
evidence of the categories, but it is not the form for Tax Year 2026-27.
Schedule FA for Assessment Year 2026-27 asks about foreign depository and
custodial accounts, foreign equity and debt interests, financial interests in
foreign entities, other capital assets, other accounts with signing authority,
and foreign-source income. It asks for the institution, country, account,
opening date, peak balance, and closing balance for a depository account, and
reports accounts held at any time in the relevant calendar-year period.
[Notification 47/2026, ITR-3 for Assessment Year 2026-27, issued 30 March
2026, Schedule
FA](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/documents/d/guest/notification-no-47-2026-pdf)

The exact ITR-3 layout, instructions, Schedule FA accounting period, and
validation rules for **Tax Year 2026-27** must be rechecked after that period's
return form and utility are published. Rule 164 already controls ITR-4
eligibility, but the prior-year schedule must not be copied into product Rules
as though it were the new form.

## Account and balance decision table

| Arrangement established from the user's documents | Tax-return fact | Product treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ordinary or EEFC account with an authorised dealer bank in India | Foreign currency does not make an India-located account a foreign account. | This fact alone does not stop the supported path. |
| Bank account outside India in the user's sole or joint name | Foreign account and asset held outside India; ordinarily also authority over that account. | Keep any independent tax estimate, suppress ITR-4 guidance, and direct the user to ITR-3/foreign-asset guidance or a tax professional. |
| Account outside India owned by another person or entity which the user can operate, authorise payments from, or sign for | Signing authority outside India even if the money is not the user's. | Same return-guidance stop. |
| “Virtual receiving” bank details where the bank or account statement identifies the user as account holder or beneficial owner of an account outside India | Foreign account; “virtual” does not change the named holder or location. | Same return-guidance stop. |
| Routing details that only direct money into a payment aggregator's pooled collection account, with no account title, bank relationship, or operating authority for the user | Use of those details alone does not establish that the user holds or signs the aggregator's account. | May continue only if proceeds settle automatically to the user's Indian bank account and no separate balance or foreign asset exists. |
| Withdrawable, spendable, or transferable balance maintained for the user by a foreign wallet, e-money provider, or platform | At least a possible foreign account or foreign asset represented by the user's contractual claim. Exact Schedule FA category and situs are not settled by the reviewed sources. | Conservative partial stop: retain tax arithmetic only when the gross rupee receipts are otherwise established; give no ITR-4 or “no foreign disclosure” conclusion. |
| Platform earnings shown as pending, escrowed, reserve-held, or available for later payout by a foreign entity | The platform's pooled account is not automatically the user's account, but the user's claim to the money may itself be an asset. The location and ownership depend on the contract. | Same partial stop unless qualified review establishes the arrangement. |
| Customer relationship with a foreign platform but no held balance, account, financial product, equity, debt investment, or operating authority | A customer or merchant relationship alone is not a financial interest in the provider. | Do not create a foreign-financial-interest fact from the brand alone. |

Two official rules explain why the distinction must follow legal facts rather
than payment-screen labels.

First, the Income-tax Rules' financial-account reporting provisions define a
depository account to include an account or notional account representing
specified electronic-money products held for a customer's benefit. That
definition appears in rule 238 for reporting financial institutions; it is not
expressly incorporated into Schedule FA or rule 164. It shows that an
e-money-style balance cannot safely be dismissed as “not a bank account,” but
it does not conclusively classify every freelancer wallet for the return.
[Income-tax Rules, 2026, rule 238(1) Explanation, notified 20 March
2026](https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/sites/default/files/2026-03/En-Notified-IT-Rules-2026-20-03-2026.pdf)

Second, RBI defines a Payment Aggregator-Cross Border escrow account as the
aggregator's pooled account for money collected for onboarded merchants. An
export PA-CB maintains its Export Collection Account with an authorised dealer
Category-I scheduled commercial bank; proceeds are credited to that account
and settled only to the onboarded merchant's account. A merchant does not
therefore acquire signing authority over the PA-CB collection account merely
by being paid through it. A separate wallet, stored balance, or named receiving
account is a different arrangement and needs its own analysis. [RBI circular
on Regulation of Payment Aggregator–Cross Border, issued 31 October 2023,
paragraphs 1.2 and
5](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=12561)

### What a provider brand cannot establish

A brand name cannot establish:

- which legal entity contracted with the user or where that entity is located;
- whether the service is an RBI-authorised PA-CB route, a direct foreign-bank
  account, e-money, a wallet, escrow, or only payment instructions;
- whether the user or the provider is the legal account holder;
- whether an account is individual, pooled, or merely a routing sub-ledger;
- whether the user can hold, spend, transfer, or invest a balance;
- whether the user can operate the underlying account or only request a payout;
- whether funds move automatically to an Indian bank or remain a claim against
  a foreign entity; or
- whether the same brand used an Indian entity for one user and a foreign
  entity or product for another.

The Application must not ask “Do you use Brand X?” and turn that answer into a
tax result. It must ask the arrangement facts below and offer “Not sure.”

## Income from a foreign client is not automatically foreign-source income

Section 5 taxes a resident on income received or accruing in India and income
accruing outside India. For an RNOR, outside-India income is included only when
derived from a business controlled in or profession set up in India. [Income-
tax Act, 2025 as amended, section
5](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/documents/d/guest/income_tax_act_2025_as_amended_by_fa_act_2026-pdf)

Section 6(14), for the residence section, defines “income from foreign sources”
as income accruing or arising outside India, except income derived from a
business controlled in or profession set up in India, and excludes income
deemed to accrue or arise in India. The definition is expressly limited to
section 6, so it is not by itself a statutory definition for rule 164. It
nevertheless directly rejects the shortcut that every foreign customer creates
foreign-source professional income. [Income-tax Act, 2025 as amended, section
6(14)](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/documents/d/guest/income_tax_act_2025_as_amended_by_fa_act_2026-pdf)

Section 9 deems income through an Indian asset, source, property, or business
connection to accrue in India and attributes business income to operations
carried out in India where operations span jurisdictions. [Income-tax Act,
2025 as amended, section
9](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/documents/d/guest/income_tax_act_2025_as_amended_by_fa_act_2026-pdf)
The Supreme Court has likewise treated the location of the operations that
produce business profits as material and distinguished accrual and receipt
from the mere place where a sale or payment occurs. The case concerned a
non-resident and an offshore/onshore contract, so it supplies a principle, not
a ruling on an Indian freelancer. [*Ishikawajma-Harima Heavy Industries Ltd v
Director of Income Tax*, Civil Appeal 9 of 2007, judgment issued 4 January
2007](https://api.sci.gov.in/jonew/judis/28453.pdf)

The product may therefore use the following conservative classification:

- **Supported Indian-source professional operations:** the practice is set up
  and managed in India; the user performs all income-producing work in India;
  there is no foreign office, fixed base, employee, agent, or subcontracted
  operation; and the only foreign fact is the customer, platform, currency, or
  permitted payment rail.
- **Fact-dependent; stop:** any work is performed while the user is outside
  India; the practice has an office, fixed base, employee, agent, or operations
  abroad; contractual rights accrue through such a foreign establishment; the
  user is RNOR; or the user cannot confirm where the work was performed and
  the practice was set up.
- **Foreign-tax stop:** any foreign tax was withheld or paid, a foreign return
  treats the receipt as locally sourced, or foreign-tax credit or treaty relief
  may be claimed. A foreign jurisdiction's source rule and a treaty can create
  a second-country tax issue even where the Indian professional operations are
  performed in India. The Application does not resolve that issue.

The location of receipt is a separate fact from the source of income. Moving a
foreign-client payment through an Indian bank does not make foreign operations
Indian; retaining an India-earned fee in a foreign account does not make the
work foreign. It can, however, create the separate foreign-account or asset
fact discussed above.

## Minimum non-identifying questions

The Application can resolve the narrow supported path with a few factual
questions. All must offer “Not sure,” which follows the stop path.

1. **Income-tax residence:** “For this tax year, are you resident and
   ordinarily resident in India?” An ordinary “resident” answer is not enough
   for ITR-4.
2. **Place of work and practice:** “Was this freelance practice set up and
   managed in India, with all work performed while you were physically in
   India?”
3. **Foreign account or authority:** “At any time in this tax year, did you
   hold, jointly hold, or have authority to operate any bank or payment account
   outside India, including virtual receiving details?”
4. **Provider-held balance:** “Could money remain in a foreign payment service
   or platform balance that you could later withdraw, spend, or transfer,
   instead of being sent automatically to your own Indian bank account?”
5. **Document check:** “Do any account documents name you as holder or
   beneficial owner of an account or balance maintained outside India?”
6. **Foreign operations:** “Did you perform any work outside India or use an
   office, fixed base, employee, agent, or subcontracted operation outside
   India?”
7. **Foreign tax:** “Was tax withheld or paid outside India, or might you claim
   foreign-tax or treaty relief?”

These questions need no provider name, client name, client country, account
number, routing code, account address, balance, invoice, tax identifier, or
upload. Do not save such identifiers. The user may inspect their own contract,
statement, and account-holder page locally to answer.

## FEMA boundary

Income-tax residence does not establish FEMA residence. FEMA section 2(v) uses
the preceding year's presence plus purpose and intention rules. The
Application should not declare an RBI arrangement compliant from an income-tax
residence answer. [Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999, Act 42 of 1999,
section 2(v), enacted 29 December 1999, current India Code consolidation
checked 2 September
2026](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/1988/1/A1999-42.pdf)

For a person resident in India under FEMA, section 8 requires reasonable steps
to realise and repatriate foreign exchange due or accrued within the RBI's
specified period and manner. “Foreign exchange due” includes an amount the
person has a right to receive or claim. [FEMA section 8](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/1988/1/A1999-42.pdf)
[Foreign Exchange Management (Realisation, Repatriation and Surrender of
Foreign Exchange) Regulations, 2015, notified 29 December
2015](https://upload.indiacode.nic.in/showfile?actid=AC_CEN_2_45_00004_199942_1517807323986&filename=showrulefile_%289%29.pdf&type=regulation)

RBI expressly permits a resident exporter to open and maintain a foreign-
currency account with a bank outside India for export value and advances.
Funds must be used for imports into India or repatriated by the end of the next
month after receipt, or within three months for an account in an IFSC, after
the stated adjustments and subject to export-realisation rules. This
permission confirms that the account can be lawful; it does not remove its
outside-India location for income-tax return rules. [FEMA 10(R)(7)/2025-RB,
issued 6 October
2025](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/BS_FemaNotifications.aspx?Id=12916)

The export regime also changes during this tax year. The 2015 export
regulations as amended require nine-month realisation through 30 September
2026. FEMA 23(R)/2026-RB takes effect on 1 October 2026 and generally uses 15
months from a service invoice, or 18 months for specified INR invoicing or
settlement, with authorised-dealer extensions. It also introduces service EDF
declarations. [RBI First Amendment to the 2015 Export Regulations, issued 5
June 2026](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/BS_FemaNotifications.aspx?Id=13467)
[Foreign Exchange Management (Export and Import of Goods and Services)
Regulations, 2026, issued 13 January 2026, Gazette 15 January 2026, effective 1
October
2026](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/BS_FemaNotifications.aspx?Id=13277)

Do not calculate FEMA compliance from a provider brand or annual receipt
total. If the user held money outside India, used a route they cannot identify
as authorised, missed a declaration, or may have delayed realisation or
repatriation, show generic guidance to take the account documents and invoices
to their authorised dealer bank or a qualified adviser.

## Safe result boundary

Use three outcomes for this part of the Profile:

1. **Fully supported:** all income-producing services and the practice are in
   India; funds settle directly through an authorised route to the user's own
   Indian bank account; and there is no foreign account, provider-held balance,
   signing authority, foreign operation, or foreign tax.
2. **Tax estimate only:** tax arithmetic and gross rupee receipts are otherwise
   valid, but the user definitely or possibly has a foreign account, wallet,
   platform balance, asset, or signing authority. Do not name ITR-4, say that
   no foreign disclosure applies, or mark the annual-return journey fully
   covered. Point to the official ITR-3/foreign-asset starting material and
   recommend qualified review.
3. **Unsupported calculation:** work or professional operations occurred
   outside India; residency subtype is RNOR or unknown; foreign tax was
   withheld or paid; relief may be claimed; or the gross rupee receipts and
   foreign-currency/account effects are unresolved. Those facts can alter
   taxable income, source allocation, credits, or return content.

An account or asset stop does not mean the arrangement was unlawful, that tax
is unpaid, or that the user failed to file. It means only that the Application
cannot support simplified-return and disclosure guidance from the facts it
collects.

## Caveats and release checks

- No official source reviewed gives a brand-by-brand classification of common
  freelancer platforms, wallets, or virtual receiving accounts.
- Rule 238's electronic-money definition is confined to financial-institution
  reporting. It supports caution but is not a direct Schedule FA definition.
- The situs of an intangible contractual claim against a platform is not
  resolved by the reviewed official materials. Do not hard-code one.
- The Indian-source treatment above assumes all income-producing professional
  operations occur in India. Mixed-location work needs allocation and possibly
  treaty analysis.
- The Tax Year 2026-27 ITR-3 form, instructions, Schedule FA period, schema, and
  validations require a release-time check when published. This belongs in the
  statutory release gate, not in a speculative local rule now.
- RBI-authorised payment routing and income-tax foreign-asset classification
  are separate questions. One does not establish the other.

## Compliance findings for the proposed expansion

- **S1 — residence subtype:** the current Profile asks only whether the user is
  a resident individual. Rule 164 does not permit an RNOR to use ITR-4.
  Smallest safe successor change: ask resident-and-ordinarily-resident status
  or suppress form guidance.
- **S1 — account ambiguity:** treating every platform or foreign-client payment
  as account-free can give wrong ITR-4 guidance. Smallest safe change: ask the
  foreign-account, authority, and holdable-balance questions above; “Yes” or
  “Not sure” takes the partial-stop path.
- **S1 — source shortcut:** treating every foreign payer as foreign-source
  income would unnecessarily reject India-based professional work; treating
  every remote contract as Indian-source would miss mixed-location operations.
  Smallest safe change: ask where the practice was set up and where all work
  was physically performed.
- **S1 — period-specific form:** importing Assessment Year 2026-27 Schedule FA
  into Tax Year 2026-27 Rules would use the wrong period's form. Smallest safe
  change: make publication and review of the Tax Year form a release gate.

No additional Wayfinder ticket is required. **Define the supported solo-
freelancer Profile** can settle the full, partial, and unsupported branches,
and **Define verification and release gates** already owns the later Tax Year
form review.
