# Income-tax treatment for solo digital freelancers

Reviewed on 2 September 2026 for Tax Year 2026-27 (1 April 2026 to
31 March 2027). This note uses the Income-tax Act, 2025 as amended by the
Finance Act, 2026, the Income-tax Rules, 2026, and current Income Tax
Department material. It does not address GST, foreign-tax credit, or income
unrelated to the supported freelance practice.

## Answer

Foreign clients and foreign-currency payment do not, by themselves, prevent a
resident individual from using a presumptive calculation under section 58.
Section 58 does not restrict the payer's country. A resident's total income also
includes income accruing in or outside India, subject to the special rule for a
resident not ordinarily resident (RNOR). This is an inference from sections 5
and 58, not an official classification of every cross-border service contract.
[Income-tax Act, 2025 as amended by Finance Act, 2026, sections 5 and 58
(issued 30 March 2026)](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/documents/d/guest/income_tax_act_2025_as_amended_by_fa_act_2026-pdf)

There are two possible presumptive paths, but occupation labels such as
"designer", "writer", "marketer", and "consultant" do not choose between
them. The first safe expansion is therefore the existing 50% professional path
for a user who affirmatively confirms that the work is a section 62 specified
profession. Supporting the broader solo-freelancer market requires a separate
product decision about whether to add the 6%/8% eligible-business path and how
the user will establish that their activity is a business rather than a
profession.

| Path | Who can use it | Receipts limit | Deemed income |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| Section 58(2), Table row 3: specified profession | Resident individual carrying on a profession in section 62(4) | ₹50 lakh; ₹75 lakh if cash receipts do not exceed 5% | 50% of gross receipts or higher profit actually claimed |
| Section 58(2), Table row 1: eligible business | Resident individual carrying on a business other than goods carriage, who does not carry on a specified profession, earn commission or brokerage, conduct agency business, or claim a Chapter VIII-C deduction | ₹2 crore; ₹3 crore if cash receipts do not exceed 5% | 6% of qualifying banking/online receipts and 8% of the balance, or higher profit actually claimed |

The limits, rates, eligible-assessees, business exclusions, and cash test are in
[section 58](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/w/section-58-138). The
consolidated Act is the controlling source if the HTML page differs.

## Profession and business classification

Section 62(4) expressly lists legal, medical, engineering, architectural,
accountancy, technical consultancy, interior decoration, information
technology, and company secretary professions, plus professions later notified
by the Board. [Section 62](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/w/section-62-134)

For the requested audience, this yields the following conservative boundary:

- Software developers and other technology freelancers can use the
  professional path only after confirming that their actual practice is the
  listed **information technology** profession. The Act does not define that
  term or provide a role-title lookup.
- A consultant can use it only if the actual practice is **technical
  consultancy**, information technology, or another listed profession. A
  generic consultant label is insufficient.
- Graphic, product, UI/UX, and other designers are not named as a class.
  Interior decoration is named. A digital designer might form part of an
  information-technology practice, but the primary sources reviewed do not make
  that automatic.
- General copywriters, content writers, and other writers are not named as a
  class. The Rules recognise story, screenplay, and dialogue writers only within
  the defined cinematograph-film profession; that does not cover general
  digital writing.
- Marketing is not a section 62 specified profession. "Advertising" appears in
  the separate definition of professional services for tax deduction at
  source, but that does not add it to section 62.

Rule 46's prescribed-books provisions retain authorised representatives and
film artists and define the narrow film-artist roles. [Income-tax Rules, 2026,
rule 46, notified 20 March 2026 and effective 1 April
2026](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/documents/d/guest/notification-22-2026-1)
The Act's repeal-and-savings rule also continues prior notifications where they
are consistent with the corresponding new provision. [Income-tax Act, 2025 as
amended, section 536(2)(j)](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/documents/d/guest/income_tax_act_2025_as_amended_by_fa_act_2026-pdf)

The 6%/8% path is not a fallback for every person who fails the specified-
profession test. The Act defines "business" and "profession" separately, and
section 62 also refers separately to a business and a non-specified profession.
The sources do not classify generic digital roles as one or the other. A role
name alone cannot safely select the business path. [Income-tax Act, 2025 as
amended, sections 2(20), 2(86), 58 and
62](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/documents/d/guest/income_tax_act_2025_as_amended_by_fa_act_2026-pdf)

If the business path is later supported, it needs two facts that the
professional path does not:

1. Whether qualifying turnover was received through a specified banking or
   online mode during the tax year or by the section 263(1) return due date;
   that portion is deemed at 6%, with the remainder at 8%.
2. Whether the person used this business presumptive path and then declared
   contrary profits in any of the succeeding five tax years; section 58(7)
   imposes a five-year exclusion. This lockout does not apply to the specified-
   profession row.

"Specified Banking or Online Mode" means account-payee cheque or draft,
electronic clearing through a bank account, or a prescribed electronic mode.
Rule 48 lists credit card, debit card, net banking, IMPS, UPI, RTGS, NEFT, and
BHIM Aadhaar Pay. Neither source expressly classifies a balance held inside a
foreign freelance platform or wallet. [Income-tax Act, 2025 as amended, section
66(32)](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/documents/d/guest/income_tax_act_2025_as_amended_by_fa_act_2026-pdf)
[Income-tax Rules, 2026, rule
48](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/documents/d/guest/notification-22-2026-1)

## Gross receipts, platforms, and withholding

All receipts of the one supported practice, domestic and foreign, must be
aggregated for the section 58 limit. The professional calculation is 50% of
**gross receipts**, not the amount left after expenses. Section 58(4) disallows
any further loss, allowance, or deduction against presumptive income.
[Income-tax Act, 2025 as amended, section
58(2)-(4)](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/documents/d/guest/income_tax_act_2025_as_amended_by_fa_act_2026-pdf)

Accordingly, where the records show that a platform facilitated the user's
service, credited the gross customer charge to the user, and separately
deducted its fee, the gross service amount belongs in gross receipts. The
platform fee must not be subtracted before applying 50%, and it is not separately
deductible afterward. For Indian e-commerce withholding, section 393 expressly
treats even a customer's direct payment as part of the gross platform-facilitated
sale or service and applies withholding to the gross amount. This supports the
pre-fee treatment for a facilitation model; it does not determine the contract
relationship of every foreign platform. [Income-tax Act, 2025 as amended,
section 393(1), Table row 8(v) and Note
3](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/documents/d/guest/income_tax_act_2025_as_amended_by_fa_act_2026-pdf)

A net bank settlement alone is insufficient when the statement does not expose
the gross service charge, platform fee, refunds, and adjustments, or when the
platform might be the principal rather than an intermediary. The application
must not reverse-engineer gross receipts from a net payout.

Indian TDS/TCS does not reduce gross receipts. It is a tax credit: Chapter
XIX-B treats tax deducted or collected and paid to the Central Government as
payment on behalf of the taxpayer. The calculation may continue with actual
Indian TDS/TCS for the included income entered separately. [Income-tax Act,
2025 as amended, section
391(5)](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/documents/d/guest/income_tax_act_2025_as_amended_by_fa_act_2026-pdf)

If a foreign payer or platform withholds foreign tax, gross receipts remain the
pre-withholding amount, but this product must stop because relief or credit for
tax paid outside India is explicitly outside scope. Sections 159 and 160 govern
that relief or deduction, and the Rules exclude such a claimant from ITR-4.
[Income-tax Act, 2025 as amended, sections 159 and
160](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/documents/d/guest/income_tax_act_2025_as_amended_by_fa_act_2026-pdf)
[Income-tax Rules, 2026, rule
164(6)](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/documents/d/guest/notification-22-2026-1)

## Timing, cash test, and foreign currency

The tax year is the financial year starting 1 April. Business/professional
income must use the cash or mercantile accounting method regularly employed by
the taxpayer. The product therefore cannot silently treat every invoice as a
receipt or every bank credit as the only relevant event. [Income-tax Act, 2025
as amended, sections 3 and
276](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/documents/d/guest/income_tax_act_2025_as_amended_by_fa_act_2026-pdf)

For the professional receipt-limit test, cash receipts at exactly 5% still
qualify for the ₹75 lakh limit; only a percentage above 5% fails it. A
non-account-payee cheque or bank draft is deemed cash. [Income-tax Act, 2025 as
amended, section 58(2), Table row 3 and section
58(9)](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/documents/d/guest/income_tax_act_2025_as_amended_by_fa_act_2026-pdf)

The official conversion rules do not justify a simple annual spot-rate feature:

- Rule 206 says income expressed in foreign currency is converted at the State
  Bank of India telegraphic-transfer buying rate on the specified date. For
  business/professional income, the table names the last day of the tax year.
  The same rule contains an exception for income received in or brought into
  India before that date, but its published text still refers to the repealed
  Foreign Exchange Regulation Act, 1973.
- Official ICDS guidance says a foreign-currency transaction is initially
  recorded at the transaction-date rate (a representative weekly or monthly
  average may be used when it approximates that rate), and foreign-currency
  monetary items are translated at the closing rate. It also says the Act or
  Rules prevail over ICDS in a conflict and that relevant ICDS rules can affect
  turnover/receipts under a presumptive scheme.

[Income-tax Rules, 2026, rules 206 and
207](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/documents/d/guest/notification-22-2026-1)
[Income Tax Department, Income Computation and Disclosure Standards, including
ICDS VI](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/en/income-computation-and-disclosure-standards-icds-1)

Rule 206 speaks of **income**, while section 58 applies a percentage to **gross
receipts**. The reviewed primary sources do not supply a safe one-rate shortcut
from annual foreign-currency billings to section 58 gross receipts, especially
where invoices remain unpaid or money remains in a foreign wallet. The minimal
safe product boundary is to accept only an annual integer-rupee gross-receipts
total that the user confirms was prepared from their tax records using their
regular accounting method. Do not fetch rates or convert currency totals in
this phase. Stop if the user has only currency totals, net settlements, open
foreign receivables, or exchange gains/losses that have not already been
reflected in that confirmed rupee total.

## Advance tax and annual return

Advance tax applies when the computed advance tax is ₹10,000 or more. A person
declaring presumptive profit under either section 58 row 1 or row 3 pays the
whole amount by 15 March 2027; an amount paid by 31 March is still treated as
advance tax for that year. [Income-tax Act, 2025 as amended, sections 404 and
408](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/documents/d/guest/income_tax_act_2025_as_amended_by_fa_act_2026-pdf)

For the supported new-regime individual, income alone requires a return when
total income before the listed deductions exceeds the ₹4 lakh maximum amount
not chargeable to tax. Independent prescribed triggers also include
professional gross receipts above ₹10 lakh, business turnover/gross receipts
above ₹60 lakh, TDS plus TCS of at least ₹25,000 (₹50,000 for a resident
individual aged 60 or more), and the other banking, travel, electricity, and
savings-deposit conditions in rule 163. A resident ordinarily resident who
holds or benefits from a foreign asset or has signing authority over a foreign
account also has a statutory filing trigger irrespective of income. [Income-tax
Act, 2025 as amended, sections 202(1) and
263(1)](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/documents/d/guest/income_tax_act_2025_as_amended_by_fa_act_2026-pdf)
[Income-tax Rules, 2026, rule
163](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/documents/d/guest/notification-22-2026-1)

The normal due date for a non-audit taxpayer with business or professional
income is 31 August 2027. An audit case is normally due 31 October 2027.
[Income-tax Act, 2025 as amended, section
263(1)(c)](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/documents/d/guest/income_tax_act_2025_as_amended_by_fa_act_2026-pdf)

Contrary to the repository's current planning assumption, Tax Year 2026-27
return forms have now been notified. Rule 164 makes ITR-4 available for eligible
section 58 presumptive taxpayers but excludes an RNOR, a person with an asset or
signing authority outside India, a person with income from a source outside
India, a person claiming foreign-tax relief, and the other listed conditions.
An individual with business/professional income who is not eligible for ITR-4
uses ITR-3. A foreign customer's location alone does not establish whether the
service income is from a source outside India, so the product must not promise
ITR-4 merely because the section 58 calculation is supported. [Income-tax
Rules, 2026, rule
164(5)-(7)](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/documents/d/guest/notification-22-2026-1)

## Expanded-profile stop conditions

The calculation must stop when any of these facts applies or cannot be
confirmed:

1. The user is not a resident individual. RNOR should also stop until the
   product deliberately supports its narrower worldwide-income and return-form
   rules.
2. The sole practice is not clearly a section 62 specified profession, unless
   the separate eligible-business calculation path is implemented and the user
   can affirm that classification.
3. The user has another business/profession, mixed specified and non-specified
   work, employment-like receipts, agency activity, or commission/brokerage
   income that the selected path does not model.
4. Gross receipts exceed ₹50 lakh where cash receipts exceed 5%, or exceed ₹75
   lakh where cash receipts do not exceed 5%, for the professional path.
5. Cash receipts are unknown, exceed gross receipts, or include payment methods
   the user cannot classify. Non-account-payee cheques and drafts count as cash.
6. The user wants to declare professional profit below 50% of gross receipts,
   or other facts require books/audit rather than the selected presumptive path.
7. The user cannot provide gross rupee receipts before Indian tax withholding
   and separately charged platform fees, using a consistently applied cash or
   mercantile method.
8. Foreign tax was withheld or foreign-tax relief/deduction is claimed.
9. Foreign-currency receivables, wallet balances, exchange gains/losses, refunds,
   chargebacks, reimbursements, or platform adjustments are not already
   resolved in the confirmed gross rupee total.
10. Any existing out-of-scope income, deduction, loss, credit, surcharge, or
    special-rate fact applies. The current ₹50 lakh total-income ceiling remains
    a necessary stop while surcharge and broader return facts are unsupported.

Foreign clients alone, a platform alone, and actual Indian TDS/TCS alone are
not stop conditions. Foreign assets/accounts and genuinely foreign-source
income do not necessarily invalidate the section 58 tax arithmetic, but they
must suppress simplified-return guidance and trigger ITR-3/foreign-disclosure
guidance that is outside the current product. If the product promises one
fully-supported plan rather than only a tax estimate, treat them as stops until
that guidance is implemented.

## Release blockers for the expansion

- **S1 — occupation classification:** Automatically mapping all developers,
  designers, writers, marketers, and consultants to the 50% professional path
  would exceed section 62. The smallest safe fix is an explicit specified-
  profession confirmation and conservative help text; stop uncertain roles.
- **S1 — foreign-currency gross receipts:** Automatic conversion from one annual
  currency total is unsupported by the reviewed sources. Accept a confirmed
  rupee total from the user's tax records; defer conversion.
- **S1 — platform net payouts:** A net payout can understate gross receipts.
  Require a gross figure from invoices/platform records and stop net-only data.
- **S1 — return guidance:** Section 58 eligibility does not guarantee ITR-4
  eligibility. Avoid naming a form unless source, residency subtype, foreign
  assets/accounts, and foreign-tax relief are known.

There are no findings against the current release's calculation from this
research: it already rejects foreign clients and non-IT work. These blockers
apply when the profile is broadened.

## Decisions now sharp enough for Wayfinder tickets

1. **Choose one or two presumptive paths for solo freelancers:** decide whether
   Phase 2 supports only section 62 specified professionals at 50%, or also the
   section 58 eligible-business 6%/8% path with classification, receipt-mode,
   and five-year-history inputs.
2. **Define the supported freelancer occupation taxonomy:** choose the exact
   plain-language activities that can reach a result, the confirmation wording,
   and the stop route for ambiguous designers, writers, marketers, and general
   consultants.
3. **Set the foreign-receipts input contract:** decide whether the app accepts
   only a user-confirmed annual rupee gross total (recommended) and how it
   explains accounting method, platform fees, refunds, and open receivables.
4. **Research foreign payment accounts and service-source classification:**
   determine when balances or signing authority in common freelance platforms,
   wallets, and receiving accounts are foreign assets/accounts, and when work
   performed in India for a foreign client is income from a source outside India
   for rule 164.
5. **Choose the return-guidance boundary:** decide whether foreign assets or
   foreign-source professional receipts stop the whole plan or only suppress
   ITR-4-specific guidance while retaining the tax estimate and generic
   31 August obligation.

## Source status and caveats

- **Controlling Act:** Income-tax Act, 2025 as amended by Finance Act, 2026,
  issued 30 March 2026 and effective for Tax Year 2026-27.
- **Controlling Rules:** G.S.R. 198(E), Income-tax Rules, 2026, notified
  20 March 2026 and effective 1 April 2026.
- The Act and Rules use several broad terms without an occupation-by-occupation
  definition. No primary source reviewed maps generic UI/UX design, writing,
  marketing, or management consulting to section 58.
- Rule 206's published FERA reference and the distinction between conversion of
  "income" and determination of "gross receipts" make automatic foreign-
  currency conversion unsuitable without a narrower, separately reviewed
  design.
- Common payment platforms are not named in the foreign-asset, foreign-account,
  source-of-income, or specified-online-mode provisions reviewed. Their legal
  treatment depends on the actual account and contract structure.
- Statutory rules and return guidance must remain expiry-bound and be rechecked
  before release.
