# GST filing calendar for FY 2026-27

Reviewed on 6 September 2026. This note supports a calendar for one active normal-taxpayer GST registration in one Indian state or Union territory. It does not establish GST payable or portal acceptance. The extension review has a limitation described below.

## Verified normal dates

| Filing or review | Normal deadline | Direct authority |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly GSTR-1 | 11th of following month | Notification 83/2020-Central Tax |
| Quarterly GSTR-1 | 13th of following quarter's first month | Notification 83/2020-Central Tax |
| Monthly GSTR-3B | 20th of following month | Rule 61, substituted by Notification 82/2020-Central Tax |
| Quarterly GSTR-3B | 22nd or 24th of following quarter's first month | Rule 61 state table, same notification |
| QRMP payment review, first and second months only | 25th of following month | Rule 61(3), same notification |

Both notifications were issued on 10 November 2020. The continuing calendar provisions commenced on 1 January 2021. Notification 83 is itself a continuing extension of the underlying section 37 date. Use 11/13 as the product's normal dates, with later period-specific extensions stored separately. [Notification 83/2020](https://gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/2024-05/notfctn-83-central-tax-english-2020.pdf), [Notification 82/2020](https://gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/2024-05/notfctn-82-central-tax-english-2020.pdf).

The Rule 61 table specifies these jurisdictions for the 22nd: Chhattisgarh, Madhya Pradesh, Gujarat, Maharashtra, Karnataka, Goa, Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Telangana, Andhra Pradesh, Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu, Puducherry, Andaman and Nicobar Islands, Lakshadweep.

The 24th applies to Himachal Pradesh, Punjab, Uttarakhand, Haryana, Rajasthan, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Sikkim, Arunachal Pradesh, Nagaland, Manipur, Mizoram, Tripura, Meghalaya, Assam, West Bengal, Jharkhand, Odisha, Jammu and Kashmir, Ladakh, Chandigarh, Delhi. The notification's wording names the former Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu territories together; use the existing merged jurisdiction in the application. Rule 61 expressly covers a month or quarter in which registration applies for only part of that period. [Notification 82/2020, paragraph 5](https://gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/2024-05/notfctn-82-central-tax-english-2020.pdf).

## Cadence and eligibility

QRMP requires preceding-year aggregate turnover no greater than ₹5 crore and the taxpayer's election. The return due when exercising that election must have been furnished. The election continues until changed or eligibility is lost. If current-year aggregate turnover exceeds ₹5 crore during a quarter, quarterly eligibility ends from the first month of the succeeding quarter. Exactly ₹5 crore does not exceed the limit. [Notification 84/2020-Central Tax, issued 10 November 2020](https://gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/2024-05/notfctn-84-central-tax-english-2020.pdf).

Ask for the cadence confirmed on the GST portal for each relevant quarter. Never infer an election from turnover. Applying today's cadence to earlier quarters would produce incorrect deadlines. Rule 61A permits selecting cadence from the second month of the preceding quarter through the final day of the quarter's first month. New registrations can elect QRMP only for a quarter whose election window remains open. A registration obtained in a quarter's second or third month therefore cannot newly elect QRMP for that same quarter. [Circular 143/13/2020-GST, paragraphs 4.1 and 4.5, issued 10 November 2020](https://gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/2024-06/circular_refund_143_11_2020.pdf).

Implementation recommendations:

- Keep each quarter independent. Missing cadence for one quarter must not erase another quarter's known calendar.
- Record when the normal registration began. An effective date and a grant date are different facts; where retrospective registration changes the first return period, ask for that portal period or isolate the first period for review.
- Include a partial first month and the corresponding quarter when applicable. Omit periods entirely before registration, including PMT-06 months.
- Accept registration before April 2026 and begin this calendar with April 2026. Do not create FY 2025-26 return records merely because their filing deadlines fall in April 2026.
- Ask users to update future cadence when they change their election. A projected annual turnover alone cannot establish the quarter of a mandatory transition.
- Keep the existing one-state restriction. An intra-year move or change of registration type needs separate review because a single state and start date do not describe that history.

## Conditional payments and nil periods

Notification 85 permits fixed-sum deposits based on earlier cash tax, but that method requires a complete preceding tax period. No deposit may be necessary when the relevant cash or credit ledger covers the liability, or liability is nil. The second month considers cumulative liability for both months. Do not calculate a fixed amount, infer a deposit from annual income, or label every PMT-06 item an unconditional payment due. [Notification 85/2020-Central Tax, issued 10 November 2020, effective 1 January 2021](https://gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/2024-05/notfctn-85-central-tax-english-2020.pdf).

Use a dated action such as reviewing the QRMP payment and paying if due. Completion must mean the user reviewed and handled the condition, without claiming a payment necessarily occurred. Self-assessment and fixed-sum methods are distinct; selecting a payment method does not change the calendar date. IFF is optional and must never become a required agenda item. [Circular 143/13/2020-GST, paragraphs 5.2 and 6](https://gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/2024-06/circular_refund_143_11_2020.pdf).

Keep recurring returns even if the user declares zero receipts. The normal-taxpayer return obligation follows the registration and period, while tax liability is a separate question. Section 39 establishes monthly or quarterly returns, including partial periods; the GST portal explicitly confirms GSTR-3B filing for periods without business. [CGST Act section 39, enacted 12 April 2017, current CBIC text](https://taxinformation.cbic.gov.in/content-page/explore-act/1000309/1000001), [GST Portal GSTR-3B guide, publication date unstated, checked 6 September 2026](https://tutorial.gst.gov.in/userguide/returns/GSTR3B.htm).

## Extensions and review window

Targeted official-domain searches found no extension applying to the April 2026 through March 2027 return periods. This is not proof that no extension exists. The GST Council's tax-notification index exposed entries only through Notification 11/2025 when checked; CBIC's current notification portal timed out. An exhaustive current national and state extension check could not be completed from these interfaces. [GST Council notification index](https://www.gstcouncil.gov.in/cgst-tax-notification), [CBIC tax portal](https://taxinformation.cbic.gov.in/).

The GST Council's April 2026 newsletter reports Notification 01/2026-Central Tax of 21 April 2026, extending the March 2026 monthly GSTR-3B date to 21 April. That period belongs to FY 2025-26 and should not alter the present calendar. The newsletter is evidence of the review limitation, not direct statutory provenance for an operative date in the application. [GST Council April 2026 newsletter](https://gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/2026-05/newsletter_april_issue.pdf).

Recommended product policy, rather than a statutory expiry: keep the GST calendar in its own rule group, verified on 6 September 2026, with a short review deadline no later than 30 September 2026. Do not inherit the income-tax group's August 2027 expiry. Label dates as normal statutory dates subject to notified extensions. Store an operative override only with its direct notification, filing form, exact period, and applicable jurisdiction. Expiry must withhold the calendar's personalized dates while preserving independently valid income-tax results and saved completion history. Recheck extensions immediately before public release and at every operational review. Future normal dates remain planning dates, not a guarantee that no extension will be announced.

## Independent conclusions

The existing GST threshold function discards its established above-threshold result when the crossing date is unknown. With complete aggregate turnover and supported compulsory-registration facts, keeping the threshold conclusion and a separate undated request to establish the liability date is consistent with the existing registration research. This is a product derivation, not a new tax rule. The rule's 30-day window still requires the date liability arose. See the existing [registration review](../../my-next-filing-solo-freelancer/research/gst-and-foreign-receipt-duties.md#registration-and-aggregate-turnover).

Do not expand the income-tax annual-return trigger logic as an incidental change to this calendar. Its current-year source review belongs to that separate behavior change.

## Compliance findings for implementation review

- S1: claiming that all operative FY 2026-27 dates were exhaustively checked would exceed this research. Complete the extension review before public release and retain the normal-date qualification.
- S1: inheriting a year-long GST expiry would leave an operational calendar relying on an old extension check. Give the calendar independent expiry and test its failure in isolation.
- S2: one cadence for the entire year would misdate quarters around elections. Preserve quarter-specific facts and completion identities by form and period.
- S2: ignoring partial registration periods or generating months before registration would create missing or false obligations. Test registration mid-month and around quarter boundaries.
- S2: unconditional QRMP payments would overstate liability. Keep payment reviews conditional and amount-free.
